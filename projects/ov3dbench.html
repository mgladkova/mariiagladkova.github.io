OV3D-Bench: A Diagnostic Benchmark for Open-Vocabulary Monocular 3D Detection

Mariia Gladkova, Neehar Peri, Ishan Khatri, Deva Ramanan, Daniel Cremers

ECCV 2026 Workshop — OpenSUN3D

Paper · Code · Dataset (coming soon)

Abstract

Despite recent progress in 3D visual understanding, evaluating open-vocabulary monocular 3D object detectors remains a significant challenge. Existing methods report strong in-domain performance, but each evaluates under a different protocol, several rely on per-image category oracles unavailable at deployment, and all collapse geometry and semantics into a single AP metric. To address these limitations, we introduce OV3D-Bench, a diagnostic benchmark that compares open-vocabulary monocular 3D detectors under deployment-realistic conditions across seven indoor and outdoor datasets. Our benchmark replaces per-image oracles with standardized dataset-level prompts and decouples detection accuracy along three axes: localization, semantic robustness, and cross-domain transfer. We evaluate seven representative detectors and find that (i) they localize well (mean TP 3D IoU 0.6) yet often mislabel a correctly localized box with a semantically adjacent category, e.g. all five detectors correctly localize the same outdoor vehicle but call it truck or large_vehicle instead of regular_vehicle; (ii) accuracy is highly sensitive to prompt phrasing, collapsing DetAny3D from 8.3 to 0.1 AP3D, while our remapping-based baseline shifts by at most 1.7; and (iii) the widely adopted target-aware protocol hides these errors, inflating DetAny3D's mAP3D 2.3× on the 196-category ScanNet-200 versus only 1.1× on the 15-category ARKitScenes. Lastly, we demonstrate that simply remapping a frozen closed-vocabulary detector's predictions with a contrastive vision-language encoder like SigLIPv2 performs competitively with recent open-vocabulary methods like DetAny3D. This indicates that geometric localization is largely mature while open-vocabulary semantics remains the primary bottleneck. We will release OV3D-Bench as a unified, deployment-ready benchmark.
