OV3D-Bench: A Diagnostic Benchmark for Open-Vocabulary Monocular 3D Detection

Mariia Gladkova, Neehar Peri, Ishan Khatri, Deva Ramanan, Daniel Cremers

ECCV 2026 Workshop — OpenSUN3D

Paper – Coming soon · Code – Coming soon · Dataset – Coming soon

Abstract

Open-vocabulary monocular 3D detectors report strong in-domain results, but each evaluates under a different protocol, several rely on per-image category oracles unavailable at deployment, and all collapse geometry and semantics into a single AP. To address this, we introduce OV3D-Bench, a diagnostic benchmark that compares open-vocabulary monocular 3D detectors under deployment-realistic conditions across seven indoor and outdoor datasets. Our benchmark replaces the per-image target-aware oracle with dataset-level prompts, revealed only at test time, and decouples detection accuracy along three axes: localization, semantic robustness, and cross-domain transfer. We evaluate seven representative detectors and find that (i) they localize well (mean TP 3D IoU 0.6) yet often mislabel a correctly localized box with a semantically adjacent category; (ii) accuracy is highly sensitive to prompt phrasing, collapsing DetAny3D from 8.3 to 0.1 AP3D as “car” becomes “a detailed high-resolution photo of a car”, while our remapping-based baseline shifts by at most 1.7; and (iii) the widely adopted target-aware protocol hides these errors, inflating DetAny3D's mAP3D by 1.9× where its dataset-level score is lowest (ScanNet-200) but only 1.1× where it is highest (ARKitScenes). Lastly, we demonstrate that simply remapping a frozen closed-vocabulary detector's predictions using a contrastive vision-language encoder such as SigLIPv2 performs competitively against recent purpose-built open-vocabulary methods. This indicates that geometric localization is largely mature, while open-vocabulary semantics remains the primary bottleneck. We release OV3D-Bench as a unified evaluation standard for open-vocabulary monocular 3D detection.

Key Findings

  1. Detectors localize well but mislabel. All five detectors correctly localize the same outdoor vehicle (mean TP 3D IoU 0.6 across the benchmark) yet call it truck, truck_cab, railed_vehicle, or large_vehicle instead of regular_vehicle — localization is largely solved, vocabulary is not.
  2. Accuracy is highly sensitive to prompt phrasing. Rewording a category name from a bare class name to an attribute-rich description collapses DetAny3D from 8.3 to 0.1 AP3D and WildDet3D by 13.2 AP3D, while our SigLIPv2 remapping baseline shifts by at most 1.7.
  3. The target-aware protocol hides these errors. Prompting each image only with its own ground-truth categories — the protocol used by recent work — inflates DetAny3D's mAP3D by 1.9× on the 196-category ScanNet-200 but only 1.1× on the 15-category ARKitScenes, concealing exactly the errors that matter most for safe deployment.

Benchmark Design

OV3D-Bench replaces the per-image category oracle used by prior work with dataset-level prompting — revealed only at test time — and decouples the standard AP3D metric into three orthogonal axes, evaluated across seven indoor and outdoor datasets (KITTI, nuScenes, Argoverse2, SUNRGBD, ScanNet-200, ARKitScenes, Hypersim).

01

Geometric Accuracy

TP 3D IoU averages 3D IoU over true-positive detections (IoU ≥ 0.5), following the nuScenes protocol, isolating localization quality from semantic correctness.

02

Semantic Robustness

Confusion matrices from Hungarian-matched boxes plus a template-based prompt-sensitivity probe (underdetailed / standard / overdetailed phrasing), summarized as a coefficient of variation.

03

Cross-Domain Generalization

mAP3D on held-out classes and unseen datasets, restricted to same-environment pairs, to measure whether a detector's vocabulary transfers outside its training distribution.

To isolate geometry from semantics, we also introduce a training-free VLM remapping baseline: we take a frozen closed-vocabulary detector (e.g. Cube R-CNN), encode each predicted 3D box's image crop with SigLIPv2, and remap it to the highest-similarity category in the target vocabulary. This lets a detector trained only on cars, pedestrians, and cyclists be evaluated on trams without retraining. We denote remapped models with an asterisk (e.g. Cube R-CNN*) throughout.

Failures Are Semantic, Not Geometric

Using OV3D-Bench

OV3D-Bench is built to drop into any open-vocabulary detector's evaluation pipeline — and, via our training-free remapping recipe, any closed-vocabulary one too. You bring predictions; the benchmark hands back three numbers that a single leaderboard mAP3D never surfaces on its own:

  • TP 3D IoU — is your model's geometry off, or its vocabulary?
  • Dataset-level mAP3D across all seven datasets under one standardized prompting protocol, so scores are directly comparable across papers instead of each using its own oracle.
  • Prompt CV — how much your score swings from rewording your own class names.

Pairing TP 3D IoU with mAP3D gives a fast diagnostic: a large gap between the two means your failures are semantic, not geometric — which tells you where to spend your next training run.

Code, evaluation scripts, and dataset splits are being prepared for public release. The plan is an installable evaluation harness that scores your predictions against all seven datasets under a single standardized protocol, plus a public leaderboard. Watch the GitHub repo for updates.
